# 🏠 Dormitory Operations Improvement

> Applying systems thinking to improve shared living through clear rules, shared ownership, and a sustainable operational workflow.

---

# 📌 Overview

Living in a shared dormitory exposed a number of recurring issues, including inconsistent cleaning, different daily routines, and unclear expectations.

Rather than treating these as personal disagreements, I viewed them as symptoms of an unclear process. My goal was to create a simple workflow that both residents could realistically follow, making shared living more predictable and reducing unnecessary friction.

---

# Problem

Over time, several recurring issues became apparent.

- Shared cleaning tasks had no clear owner.
- Daily routines frequently conflicted.
- Small inconveniences accumulated because they were rarely discussed immediately.
- Expectations existed, but they were never explicitly defined.

As a result, the same problems continued to repeat.

---

# Goal

Design a lightweight operational workflow that would:

- Clearly define responsibilities
- Reduce unnecessary conflicts
- Improve day-to-day communication
- Make shared-space maintenance sustainable

---

# Analysis

| Problem | Root Cause |
|----------|------------|
| Shared spaces were cleaned inconsistently | Ownership was unclear |
| Daily routines conflicted | No shared expectations |
| Frustrations accumulated | No communication process |
| Cleaning quality varied | No repeatable workflow |

---

# Solution

## Shared Living Rules

Instead of creating a long list of restrictions, we agreed on a few practical rules that could realistically be followed.

- Finish all personal routines, including showering, before **12:00 AM**
- Go to bed by **12:00 AM**
- Alternate responsibility for cleaning shared areas each week
- Communicate concerns as soon as they arise

The objective was consistency rather than complexity.

---

## Responsibility Allocation

Recurring tasks were assigned in advance to eliminate uncertainty.

### My Responsibilities

- Room floor
- Bathroom floor
- Sink
- Drain

### Roommate's Responsibilities

- Toilet
- Bathroom mirror

Private areas remained each person's own responsibility.

# Implementation

## Process Design

Each recurring task was assigned to a specific owner.

This removed questions such as:

> "Whose responsibility is this?"

and replaced assumptions with a predictable routine.

---

### Observations

- Shared spaces were maintained inconsistently.
- Cleaning depended on individual initiative.
- Responsibilities were often assumed rather than defined.

---

### Improvements

- Shared spaces became consistently cleaner.
- Ownership became clear.
- Routine maintenance became easier to sustain.
- Fewer reminders and discussions were needed.

---

# Key Design Decisions

## 1. The problem was the process—not the people.

The recurring issues were not primarily caused by individual behavior.

The real problem was the lack of a simple operating process that everyone understood.

---

## 2. Simplicity over complexity.

Adding more rules would not necessarily improve the situation.

Instead, we focused on creating a small number of agreements that everyone could realistically maintain.

---

## 3. Clear ownership reduces conflict.

Assigning every recurring task to a specific owner removed ambiguity and significantly reduced unnecessary discussions.

Ownership created accountability without requiring constant reminders.

---

## 4. Good operations prevent recurring problems.

Rather than solving the same issues repeatedly, the goal was to build a workflow that made those issues less likely to happen again.

In other words, improving the process proved more effective than repeatedly addressing individual incidents.

---

# Lessons Learned

This experience strengthened several ideas that I believe also apply to product management.

- Many recurring problems originate from unclear systems rather than people.
- Well-defined ownership improves accountability.
- Simple workflows are easier to sustain than complicated rules.
- Small operational improvements can significantly improve collaboration over time.

# Future Improvements

If this shared living arrangement had continued longer, I would have explored several additional improvements.

- Weekly cleaning checklist
- Task completion tracking
- Short weekly retrospectives
- Periodic rotation of responsibilities
- Shared inventory management for cleaning supplies
